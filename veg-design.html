🌽🌻🍅 Beds 1-3 — Tall Sun-Lovers

Corn (Ottofile), sunflowers, San Marzano + Costoluto Fiorentino tomatoes, Borlotto pole beans, Florence fennel. These plants provide the vertical structure and create micro-shade for lower beds to their south. Pole beans fix nitrogen. Sunflowers mine subsoil minerals. Tomatoes are heavy feeders — follow with fava beans in autumn.

Direct sow: Corn, beans, sunflowers, fennel — yes. Tomatoes — start indoors Feb-Mar.

🫑🍆🥒 Beds 4-6 — Medium Height

Peppers (Corno di Toro), eggplant (Violetta Lunga), zucchini (Zucchino Romanesco), squash, cucumber (Lungo Verde), basil (Genovese), chickpeas (Ceci della Toscana). Chickpeas are nitrogen fixers and dry-farmed — zero irrigation. Zucchini/squash leaves shade soil, suppress weeds. Basil repels pests for tomatoes above.

Direct sow: Zucchini, squash, cucumber, basil, chickpeas — yes. Peppers, eggplant — start indoors Mar.

🥬🌿🥗 Beds 7-9 — Low & Leafy

Cavolo Nero (Tuscan black kale), chard (Bietola), lettuce (Lattuga Romana), radicchio (Treviso), spinach (Merlo Nero). These are cut-and-come-again crops — harvest outer leaves, plant regrows. Cavolo Nero is THE Tuscan winter staple — frost improves flavor. Year-round production with successional sowing.

Direct sow: All — yes. Successional sowing every 3 weeks for continuous harvest.

🥕🧅🧄 Beds 10-12 — Roots & Alliums

Carrots (Nantas, Tonda di Parigi), red onion (Cipolla di Tropea), garlic (Aglio Bianco Piacentino), fava beans, cucumber. Fava beans are the powerhouse — nitrogen fixers (100-200 kg N/ha), winter green manure, spring food crop. Garlic biofumigates soil. Carrots NATURALLY TILL the soil with their taproots.

Direct sow: All — yes. Carrots MUST be direct sown (transplanting always fails). Fava beans sow Oct-Nov for winter cover. Garlic plant Oct, harvest Jun.

Best soil match: Silty-sand is PERFECT for carrots and garlic — loose, well-drained, no rocks. These are the soil's best friends.

🔄 Compost — 3-Bin System

🌱 How it helps the soil: The ENGINE of soil building. Every kg of compost returns organic matter, nutrients, and microbiology to the soil. Without compost, the 30cm organic layer can't be maintained. Comfrey leaves (plant on berms) are the BEST compost activator — high in nitrogen and minerals.
Bin 1: Fresh (kitchen scraps + garden waste + comfrey)
Bin 2: Maturing (turn from bin 1)
Bin 3: Ready to use (dark, earthy smell, 6 months old)
Target: 10-20m³ compost/year for 500m² vegetable garden
