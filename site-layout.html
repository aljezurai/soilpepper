This 1-hectare Tuscan permaculture site is designed for a south-facing slope at ~300m altitude. Zones radiate outward from the building. Water flows north (high) to south (low). Click any colored area for detailed information.

Quick stats: 1 ha (100×100m) 64+ fruit trees 12 vegetable beds 13 tree guilds 25 olive trees 4 swales 4 water sources 6 chickens 2-4 beehives

🏠 Zone 0 — Building (250m²)

Position: Mid-slope centerSouth-facingAbove frost line

South-facing stone building positioned mid-slope for passive solar gain and cold air drainage. Roof catchment feeds 3× 5000L rainwater tanks (~170,000L/yr harvest potential). Greywater system irrigates Zone 1. South wall used for espalier fruit trees and potted citrus. Thermal mass (stone + tile) stores daytime heat. Main living areas + terrace on south side; storage/utility on cooler north side.

Design principles: Building as solar collector, thermal battery, and windbreak for garden below. No solid walls across slope — allow cold air to drain past.

🥬 Zone 1 — Vegetable Garden (~500m²)

12 raised bedsNo-digFull sunDrip irrigation

12 no-dig raised beds (20-30cm high, 1.2m wide) in the warmest, most accessible spot — immediately south of the building. Drip irrigation gravity-fed from rainwater tanks and well.

49 Vegetables: Tomato (4), pepper (3), aubergine, zucchini (2), cucumber, pumpkin, butternut squash, courgette flowers, lettuce (4), spinach, chard, kale, cabbage (2), broccoli, cauliflower, fennel, leek, onion (3), garlic, carrot (3), parsnip, beetroot (2), radish (2), potato (3), sweet potato, celery, celeriac, pea (2), bean (3), broad bean, artichoke, asparagus, rhubarb, chicory, endive.

8 Cover Crops: White clover, crimson clover, vetch, field peas, buckwheat, phacelia, winter rye, mustard.

Herb spiral: Rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage, mint, lemon balm, basil, parsley, chives, tarragon, dill, coriander, lovage, marjoram, savory.

🍎 Zone 2 — Orchard West (~1000m²)

Cooler west aspectLate-bloomingSwales 1-2

Cooler west-facing aspect, planted with late-blooming varieties to avoid frost. Trees on swale berms access captured water. Berry hedgerow on south side provides afternoon shade benefit.

12 species (West): Apple (3 heritage), pear (2), plum (2), peach (2), apricot, cherry (2).

Tree Guilds (6):
• Apple guild: comfrey (mineral accumulator), chives + garlic (pest repellent), white clover (N-fixer), daffodils (rodent repellent)
• Pear guild: borage (pollinator attractor), yarrow (mineral accumulator), oregano (ground cover), crimson clover
• Plum guild: comfrey, nasturtium (trap crop), fava beans, calendula (pest repellent)
• Peach guild: chives, garlic, clover, tansy (ant deterrent)
• Apricot guild: borage, yarrow, clover, marigold
• Cherry guild: comfrey, chives, clover, foxglove (deep taproot)

🍑 Zone 2 — Orchard East (~1000m²)

Warm east-south aspectEarly bloomers upslopeSwales 3-4

Warmer east-south-facing aspect. Early bloomers (apricot, peach) planted higher on slope for frost drainage. Fig near building for south-wall heat retention. Berry hedgerow: heat-tolerant types along southern edge.

12 species (East): Fig, pomegranate, persimmon, grape (table + wine), lemon (marginal at 300m, south wall), almond, hazelnut, walnut, jujube, mulberry, carob, pistachio.

Tree Guilds (7):
• Fig guild: comfrey, borage, clover, nasturtium
• Pomegranate guild: chives, oregano, yarrow, clover
• Persimmon guild: comfrey, garlic, clover, calendula
• Almond guild: borage, clover, daffodils, yarrow
• Grape guild: chives, basil, clover, borage (under vine)
• Hazelnut guild: comfrey, currants, clover, garlic
• Walnut guild: comfrey, daffodils, chives, clover (tolerant species only)

Total: 13 tree guilds with 19 companion species.

🫐 Zone 2b — Berries & N-fixers

Between orchard & woodlandSwales 2-3East side

Transition zone between orchard (Zone 2) and managed woodland (Zone 4). Dense berry production combined with nitrogen-fixing shrubs that build soil and support the orchard below.

Berries (8): Goji berry, elderberry, blackberry, raspberry (autumn), red currant, black currant, gooseberry, jostaberry.

N-fixers (4): Sea buckthorn (berries + N-fix), goumi (berries + N-fix), Siberian pea shrub, amorpha (lead plant).

Cover crops under berries: Sulla (honey plant), white clover, crimson clover, vetch.

🫒 Zone 3 — Olive Grove & Forest Garden (~4000m²)

25 olive trees8m × 8m spacingCover cropped

Main productive zone with Tuscan olive grove at its heart. Olive varieties: Frantoio, Leccino, Moraiolo, Pendolino (Tuscan classics). Cover crops between rows: vetch, white clover, fava beans for nitrogen fixation and green manure.

Understory: Asparagus, artichoke, perennial herbs (rosemary, thyme, sage, oregano) along swale edges. Chestnuts (6 trees, 10m × 10m) on cooler north edge of this zone. Grain/legume rotation areas for subsistence — farro (Tuscan heritage grain), chickpeas, lentils.

Firebreak transition: Irrigated green buffer with fire-resistant species (opuntia/succulents, aloe, citrus) on SE boundary separating Zone 3 from wild vegetation.

🌲 Zone 4 — Managed Woodland (~2500m²)

North/northwest boundaryWindbreakCoppice

Primary windbreak on the NW boundary — reduces water loss by 25-40%. Planted with wind-hardy species that also provide forage, firewood, and timber.

Windbreak species: Eleagnus × ebbingei (evergreen, N-fixer), Italian alder (fast-growing, N-fixer), black locust (rot-resistant timber, N-fixer), Spanish broom (N-fixer, pioneer).

Forage trees: Oak (acorns for pigs/chickens), carob (high-energy pods), mulberry (fruit + chicken fodder).

Management: Coppice rotation for firewood (alder, locust). Annual deadwood removal for fire safety. Boar-proof fence runs through this zone on the north boundary.

🌿 Zone 5 — Wild Corridor (~1000m²)

Steepest north boundaryNo interventionBiodiversity reservoir

Unaltered native Mediterranean maquis — the biodiversity engine of the site. No intervention, no harvesting, no management except monitoring for fire risk and invasive species.

Native species: Myrtle (myrtus communis), arbutus (corbezzolo), tree heather, broom, holm oak, juniper, phillyrea, viburnum, lentisk (pistacia lentiscus).

Ecological services: Supports beneficial insects, native pollinators, birds, and natural pest predators. Wildlife corridor connecting to adjacent woodland. Seed bank for Zone 4 regeneration.

💧 Irrigation Pond (200m³)

NW corner, high point~10m × 12m × 2mGravity-fed: 0.8 bar

Located at the highest point of the property for gravity-fed drip irrigation to all zones below. Sealed with bentonite clay or EPDM liner (silty-sand subsoil won't hold water naturally). Winter refill (Oct-May, 800mm rain) fills from slope runoff via swale channel.

Multi-function: Irrigation reservoir, aquatic ecosystem, fire water reserve, wildlife drinking source. Optional: 2-4 ducks for slug control (duck manure enriches pond fertility). Solar pump from well tops up during extreme drought.

🏊 Natural Pool (15m × 5m × 1.5m)

112m³No chemicals30% regeneration zoneBetween house & garden

Chemical-free swimming pool with biological filtration through aquatic plants. Positioned between house and vegetable garden for maximum visual enjoyment and microclimate benefit.

Regeneration zone (30%): Water lilies, yellow iris, reed mace (typha), water mint, watercress, hornwort (oxygenator). The plant root zone filters and cleans the water naturally — no pumps or filters needed beyond a small solar circulation pump.

Sector responses: ☀️ South-facing for solar heating. 💧 Thermal mass moderates surrounding microclimate. 🔥 Emergency water source for firefighting. 👁️ Visible from house terrace — aesthetic centerpiece. Evaporation ~45 m³/yr topped up by rainwater + well.

🛢️ Rainwater Tanks (3 × 5000L)

15,000L totalRoof catchment~170,000L/yr potential

Three 5000L polyethylene tanks positioned uphill (north) of the house. Fed from 250m² roof via gutters with first-flush diverter. Gravity-fed to Zone 1 vegetable garden for drip irrigation.

System design: First-flush diverter sends first 100L of each rain event to garden (washes roof). Overflow directed to swale system. Tanks linked in series with ball-valve equalization. Annual harvest potential: 250m² × 670mm usable rain × 0.9 coefficient ≈ 150,000L. Tanks provide 16-minute fire response at 300L/min before well/pump kicks in.

🌀 Well (existing borehole)

Existing boreholeBackup for all systemsSolar pump

The drought insurance for the entire system. Existing borehole between house and orchard, equipped with solar-powered submersible pump.

Water security role: Primary drinking water source (filtration required). Solar pump pushes water uphill to irrigation pond during drought periods. Also fills rainwater tanks when empty and tops up natural pool evaporation (~45 m³/yr). Electric backup pump for emergency firefighting use.

Water budget: Well supplements the 4-source system: rainwater harvest (roof) + rainwater capture (swales/pond) + natural pool + well = full water security across all seasons.

🔄 Compost Area — 3-bin System

1m³ per binNorth of Zone 1Shaded

Three-bin hot composting system (1m³ each) on the north side of Zone 1 — shaded, near kitchen for easy access. Fresh material → maturing → ready.

Materials: Comfrey leaves as activator (comfrey is a mineral accumulator planted throughout guilds). Kitchen scraps, garden waste, chicken manure from coop bedding. Second compost area at orchard edge for larger prunings and woodier material (slow compost). Estimated yield: 2-3 m³ finished compost per year.

🐔 Chickens (6 Hens)

Stationary coopMovable electric nettingZone 1-2 boundary

Stationary secure coop with movable electric netting for rotational grazing. Rotated through orchard post-harvest (pest control, fertilization, weed management). Winter rotation through vegetable garden (clearing spent crops, eating pest larvae).

Breeds: Livorno (Italian heritage, good foragers) or dual-purpose mix. Estimated yield: 5-6 eggs/day in season, ~dozen/week in winter. Manure collected from coop bedding → compost system. Integrated pest management — chickens eat olive fly pupae, codling moth larvae, and snail eggs.

🐝 Beehives (2-4 Hives)

South-facing, shelteredMorning sunNear orchard

2-4 Top-bar or Langstroth hives positioned in warm, south-facing, wind-sheltered location with morning sun exposure. Critical for pollination of cherry, apple, pear, and almond trees.

Estimated yield: 20-60 kg honey per year depending on season and wildflower availability. Secondary products: beeswax, propolis, pollen. Diverse forage from all zones — orchard blossoms, olive flowers, berry blooms, wild maquis, cover crop flowers. Sulla (honey plant in Zone 2b) is an excellent bee forage species.
