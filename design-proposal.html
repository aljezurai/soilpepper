▶ 📋 Design Brief ✅ Complete

Site Overview

Location: Tuscany, Italy — ~300m altitude, hilly terrain, south-facing slope

Area: 1 hectare (10,000m²)

Climate: Csa (Hot-summer Mediterranean) — USDA Zone 8b–9a

Soil: Silty-sandy, fertile, good drainage, some clay lenses

Rainfall: ~800mm/year, concentrated Oct–Dec and Mar–May; summer drought (Jul ~25mm)

Building: 250m² south-facing stone house — roof catchment for rainwater

Frost-free period: ~180–210 days (last frost ~Apr, first frost ~Nov)

Design Goals

  • Self-sufficiency: Vegetable garden for 2 people, fruit trees, berry bushes, herbs
  • Water autonomy: Well + rainwater harvesting (15m³ tanks) + irrigation pond (200m³) + natural pool (112m³)
  • Fire safety: SE firebreak, fire-resistant species, water access for firefighting
  • Biodiversity: Native species, wildlife corridor, pollinator habitat, 7-layer forest garden
  • Integration: Chickens (6 hens), bees (2–4 hives), greywater system, compost cycling
  • Low maintenance: No-dig beds, direct sow, perennial vegetables, self-regulating guilds

Core Principles

  • Every plant must have mutual soil benefit
  • Vegetables: 30cm organic topsoil, Trees: 50cm
  • Minimal digging, direct sow preferred
  • Practical paths, no crop rotation
  • Key factors: sun, wind, height, width at maturity

▶ 🌍 Sector Analysis Summary ✅ Complete

The site's south-facing slope at ~300m altitude in Tuscany creates a unique set of sector influences. 9 sectors — 6 directional and 3 topographical — define the design constraints and opportunities. The south-facing aspect is the dominant advantage: maximum solar gain, natural shelter from cold north winds, and excellent frost drainage.

▶ 💧 Water Strategy ✅ Complete

Four water sources provide excellent water security for the 1ha site. The system is designed around gravity-fed irrigation, with the irrigation pond at the highest point of the property. Annual rainfall on the 1ha is 8,000 m³ — even a fraction captured by swales makes the system robust.

🌧️ Rainwater Harvesting
250m² roof × 800mm × 0.85 = ~170,000L/yr. 3× 5,000L tanks (15m³ total). Gravity-fed to Zone 1. First-flush diverter.
🏞️ Irrigation Pond
200m³ at highest point. Gravity head ~10m → 0.98 bar pressure. Sealed with bentonite/liner. Doubles as firefighting reserve.
🏊 Natural Pool
15×5×1.5m = 112m³. Swimming + thermal mass + irrigation reserve. With aquatic plants for natural filtration.
⛰️ Well
Existing, confirmed. Primary household water. Backup irrigation during drought years. Variable output.

Swale System

4 contour swales (~50cm deep, 1m wide) spaced at ~2.5m vertical intervals across the slope. Each swale has a berm on the downhill side where trees are planted. Swales intercept surface runoff, spread it across the slope, and recharge groundwater. Total water harvesting: hundreds of thousands of liters per year.

Gravity Drip Irrigation

Orchard and vegetable garden use on-line dripper lines with pressure compensation. Each fruit tree: 2–4 drippers at 4–8 L/h. Vegetable beds: 16mm drip tape with 30cm emitter spacing. Automated with timer (battery/solar). Summer irrigation is essential — only ~25mm rain in July.

▶ 🛤️ Access & Infrastructure ✅ Complete

Access

  • Vehicle access: From the north (existing road on woodland edge). Access road wide enough for fire truck (3.5m+).
  • Parking: North side of the building, screened from main view.
  • Pedestrian paths: Practical paths connecting house → garden → orchard → pond. No formal paths in wild zones.
  • Slope access: Gentle gradient paths follow contours where possible, stairs on steeper sections.

Infrastructure

  • 🧰 Toolshed: Near the Zone 1–2 boundary, easily accessible from house and garden.
  • 🐔 Chicken coop: Stationary secure coop at Zone 1–2 boundary, with movable electric netting for rotation.
  • 🐝 Beehives: 2–4 hives in south-facing, wind-sheltered spot with morning sun.
  • 🔄 Compost system: 3-bin system (1m³ each) on north side of Zone 1 (shaded, near kitchen). Second compost area at orchard edge for prunings.
  • 🧱 Cold frames: On south wall of building for season extension.
  • 🌿 Herb spiral: Adjacent to kitchen door — rosemary, thyme, sage, oregano, lavender, mint, parsley.
  • 🚧 Fencing: Boar-proof fence on woodland boundary. Deer protection for young trees (tree guards, first 3 years).

▶ 🔥 Fire Safety ✅ Complete

Wildfire risk is REAL in Tuscany. The combination of hot, dry summers (Jul–Sep peak), dry Mediterranean vegetation, and the Sirocco wind from the SE creates a significant fire sector. The south-facing slope also means vegetation is drier and more combustible.

🛡️
SE Firebreak Zone
0–10m cleared strip on the SE boundary. Deciduous trees (lower flammability) + mown green strip. Irrigated green buffer acts as firebreak.
🌿
Fire-Resistant Species
Near structures: Olive, Fig, Mulberry, Citrus, Aloe, Lavender, Rosemary, Sage, Iceplant, Pomegranate, Strawberry Tree. Avoid pines, eucalyptus, junipers, cypress.
💧
Water for Firefighting
Irrigation pond (200m³) on SE side serves as water source for firefighting. Natural pool (112m³) as secondary reserve. Access road wide enough for fire truck (3.5m+).
🌊
Humidity Buffer
Pond and swales on the SE side create humidity buffer against Sirocco drying. Well-watered Zone 1 (vegetable garden) on SE side of building acts as green firebreak.
🔥
Fuel Load Management
Zone 4 managed woodland — keep fuel load low. Compost pile shaded and moist in summer. No flammable debris near structures. Regular pruning and clearance.
🏠
Building Protection
Stone construction (non-combustible). Defensible space of 10m around building. Ember-resistant vents. Fire-resistant roof materials.

▶ 📅 Implementation Phasing ✅ Complete

Three-year phased implementation prioritising infrastructure first (water, access, fire safety), then production systems (orchard, guilds), then maturation and fine-tuning.

Year 1 — Infrastructure & Foundation
  • 💧 Water: Install rainwater tanks (3× 5,000L) with first-flush diverters. Dig irrigation pond (200m³). Install well pump if needed.
  • 🌊 Swales: Dig 4 contour swales with berms (~50cm deep, 1m wide).
  • 🥬 Vegetable garden: Establish 12 no-dig raised beds (30cm organic topsoil). Install drip irrigation. Plant cool-season crops.
  • 🔥 Firebreak: Clear SE firebreak zone. Plant fire-resistant hedge.
  • 🛤️ Access: Grade access road. Build pedestrian paths. Install parking area.
  • 🏠 Infrastructure: Toolshed, chicken coop, 3-bin compost system. Greywater system from house to Zone 1.
  • 🌱 Soil prep: Green manure cover crops on orchard areas. Sheet mulching on future beds.
Year 2 — Orchard, Guilds & Forest Garden
  • 🍎 Orchard: Plant 116 tree/shrub species in their assigned positions (frost/fire/wind/sun optimised). Install tree guards.
  • 🌳 Guilds: Establish 13 tree guilds with inner companions (comfrey, chives, garlic, daffodils).
  • 🌿 Zone 2b: Plant 15-species forest garden (7 layers) with 4 swales. SE firebreak planting.
  • 🫐 Berry hedgerows: Plant perimeter hedgerows (raspberry, currant, blackberry, aronia, elderberry).
  • 🐝 Bees: Install 2–4 hives in south-facing, sheltered spot.
  • 🫒 Olive grove: Plant 25 olive trees (Frantoio, Leccino, Moraiolo, Pendolino). Cover crop between rows.
  • 🏊 Natural pool: Excavate and line 15×5×1.5m natural pool with aquatic plants.
  • 🌲 Zone 4: Plant windbreak (Eleagnus, Italian alder, black locust, broom). Forage trees (oak, carob, mulberry).
Year 3+ — Maturation & Fine-Tuning
  • 🌿 Maturation: Guilds mature — mid-ring companions (clover, yarrow, oregano, strawberries). Outer ring fills in.
  • 🔄 Soil building: Sheet mulching, chop-and-drop from comfrey, green manure rotations.
  • 🐔 Chicken rotation: Full integration — orchard post-harvest, winter garden clearing, pest control.
  • 🌱 Propagation: Expand from own seed/divisions. Plant cuttings from guild companions.
  • 📊 Monitoring: Track species success, adjust siting, observe microclimate patterns.
  • 🌿 Zone 5: Establish wild corridor with native maquis species (myrtle, arbutus, heather).
  • 🔧 Fine-tuning: Adjust irrigation timing, prune for shape, replace any losses, expand productive areas.
  • 🔄 Cycling: Compost system reaches steady state. Full nutrient cycling operational.

▶ 🧠 Design Rationale ✅ Complete

Key design decisions and the reasoning behind them. Each choice is rooted in the site's specific conditions: climate, topography, soil, and the client's goals for self-sufficiency and resilience.

🏠 Why is the building south-facing?
The south-facing slope maximises passive solar gain (~30% more radiation than flat land), provides natural shelter from cold NW winds (Tramontana), and allows frost drainage downhill. The building's south wall is the warmest microclimate — ideal for season extension and tender plants.
🍎 Why are frost-sensitive trees on the upper slope?
Cold air is heavier than warm air — it flows downhill like water. The upper part of the slope is 2–4°C warmer on frost nights than the valley bottom. Peaches, apricots, and almonds (early bloomers) go highest.
💧 Why is the pond at the highest point?
Gravity-fed drip irrigation requires elevation head. The pond at the highest point provides ~8–10m head = 0.78 bar pressure — sufficient for drip irrigation without pumps. This is a permanent energy-free solution.
🌊 Why 4 swales, not more?
4 swales at ~7m vertical intervals on a 10% slope (total ~100m length) provide adequate water harvesting for the 1ha site. More swales would over-engineer the system; fewer would miss the water-spreading benefit. Each swale captures ~50–200L per meter.
🔥 Why the SE firebreak?
The Sirocco wind from the SE (135°) is the primary fire risk vector — hot, dry, and carries embers. The firebreak + pond on the SE side creates a defensible space. The irrigated vegetable garden on the south side acts as an additional green firebreak.
🌳 Why 13 tree guilds with 3-ring structure?
The 3-ring structure (inner/mid/outer) creates a self-regulating ecosystem around each tree. Inner ring: mineral accumulation + pest repellent. Mid ring: N-fixation + ground cover. Outer ring: edge habitat + pollination. This reduces maintenance by 60–80% vs. conventional orchard.
🥬 Why no-dig raised beds?
No-dig preserves soil structure, fungal networks, and earthworm populations. 30cm of organic topsoil is sufficient for deep-rooted vegetables. Raised beds warm faster in spring, improve drainage on silty-sand, and provide clear paths for maintenance.
🐝 Why chickens AND bees?
Chickens provide pest control, fertilization, and egg production. Bees provide pollination (critical for 90% of fruit trees) and honey. Together they create a closed-loop system: chickens eat pests, bees pollinate crops, manure feeds plants.
🌿 Why Zone 2b forest garden?
The 7-layer forest garden (15 species) maximises vertical space on the slope, creating diverse microclimates. The SE firebreak transition zone doubles as a productive edge. 4 swales on contour provide water to the entire system without irrigation.
📐 Why Tuscan heritage varieties?
Tuscan cultivars (Frantoio, Leccino, Moraiolo, Pendolino for olives; Brogiotto Nero for figs; Cavolo Nero for kale) are proven over centuries of local adaptation. They have better disease resistance, drought tolerance, and flavour than imported varieties.
